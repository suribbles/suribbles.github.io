Alabama Power was generating far more electricity than anyone was using. It needed people to plug in irons, washing machines, refrigerators and radios. So in 1926 it gave electricity a face: Reddy Kilowatt, a cheerful figure made of lightning bolts, with a lightbulb nose and the tagline “Your Electric Servant.” Utilities across America, and later other countries, licensed him — and American homes roughly doubled their electricity use by the late 1930s.
